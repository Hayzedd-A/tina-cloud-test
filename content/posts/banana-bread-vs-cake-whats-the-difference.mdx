Walk into any café in Lagos and you'll see "banana cake" and "banana bread" used interchangeably. They're not the same thing. Here's why the distinction matters — and why we call ours bread.

## The main difference: leavening

Cake relies on beaten air (creaming butter and sugar) and often a combination of baking powder and eggs to rise light and tall. Banana bread uses baking soda, activated by the acidity in ripe bananas. The result is a denser, more compact crumb that holds moisture far better than cake.

## Texture

A good banana cake is light and fluffy — that's the point. A good banana bread is moist and dense, with a slight chew. It doesn't crumble when you bite it. This density is what makes banana bread last two to three days without going stale in the same way a slice of cake would.

## Fat content

Traditional banana bread uses oil or melted butter, which keeps the crumb moist even after cooling. Cake typically uses room-temperature creamed butter, which gives it that smooth texture fresh out of the oven but dries out faster.

## Sweetness

Banana bread is usually less sweet than cake — the banana does the heavy lifting. At Gourmet Twist, we lean into this: our base is more banana than sugar, which is why the fruit flavour is so pronounced and why it doesn't leave you with a sugar crash.
